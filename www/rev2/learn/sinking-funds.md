# Sinking funds — the fix for bills that ambush you

Source: https://lithifyte.com/learn/sinking-funds
Updated: 2026-08-09
Licence: content CC-BY-4.0 · software AGPL-3.0-or-later

Car insurance is not a surprise. It arrives every year, on a date you know. A sinking fund converts every predictable-but-irregular bill into a flat monthly amount, which is the single highest-return change most people can make to how they handle money.

## The problem, stated exactly

Look at a year of spending and it usually shows eight or nine ordinary months and three or four that look catastrophic. The catastrophic ones contain car insurance, motor tax, a service, Christmas, a holiday deposit, the dentist.

Every one of those was known about in advance. None of them was an emergency. Yet they get handled like emergencies — on a credit card, or by raiding savings — because the budget was built around a monthly rhythm and these costs do not have one.

A sinking fund fixes this with division.

## How it works

1. List every cost that arrives less often than monthly.
2. Estimate each one's annual total.
3. Add them up and divide by twelve.
4. Move that amount into a separate account every month, and treat it as spent.
5. When a bill lands, pay it from that account.

That is the entire method. It is not clever, and its effect is out of all proportion to its complexity.

## A worked example

| Cost | Per year |
|---|---|
| Car insurance | €640 |
| Motor tax | €280 |
| Car service and NCT | €420 |
| Home insurance | €310 |
| Christmas | €800 |
| Birthdays | €300 |
| Dentist and optician | €250 |
| Annual subscriptions | €180 |
| Holiday | €1,200 |
| **Total** | **€4,380** |
| **Per month** | **€365** |

€365 a month is a real cost of running this household. It was always €365 a month. The only thing that changes is whether it is acknowledged in advance or discovered four times a year with a jolt.

And notice what the exercise reveals on its own: many people have never added these up, and the total is frequently larger than their grocery bill. That is worth knowing regardless of what you do next.

## Sinking fund versus emergency fund

They are constantly confused, and they behave completely differently.

| | Sinking fund | Emergency fund |
|---|---|---|
| Covers | Known costs, unknown-ish amounts | Unknown events |
| Timing | You know roughly when | You cannot know |
| Gets used | Several times a year, by design | Rarely, hopefully |
| When empty | Expected — it just refilled and paid out | A problem to fix |
| Target size | The next twelve months of irregular bills | Months of essential spending |

If the same "emergency" happens every year, it is not an emergency. It is a sinking-fund item that has been misfiled, and refiling it removes it from the list of things that can frighten you.

## Practical set-up

**One account or several?** One account and a written list is enough for most people, and far more likely to survive contact with real life than a dozen sub-accounts. If your bank offers named pots or spaces at no cost, use them — the labels genuinely help — but do not let the tooling become the reason you never start.

**When to move it.** The day after payday, by standing order. Money that requires a monthly decision is money that eventually does not move.

**Starting mid-year.** If insurance is due in three months and costs €640, you need roughly €213 a month to get there, not €53. Fund the near-term items at their real rate and let the distant ones build slowly; after the first full cycle, everything settles into the flat monthly figure.

**Reviewing it.** Once a year, or whenever a renewal quote changes materially. Insurance quotes drift, subscriptions creep, and a sinking fund built on last year's prices runs a little short.

## The knock-on effects

Three things happen once this is running, and the second and third are the ones people do not anticipate.

**Your monthly budget becomes honest.** The remaining categories are genuinely monthly, so the median of a normal month actually describes a normal month.

**Renewals stop being emotional.** When the insurance money is already sitting there, you can shop around calmly instead of accepting whatever renewal quote arrives while feeling ambushed. People who fund their renewals in advance tend to pay less for them, purely because they are negotiating from a position of not needing to.

**Your emergency fund stops leaking.** Most emergency funds are drained by things that were never emergencies. Take the predictable costs out and the emergency fund can finally do its actual job.
